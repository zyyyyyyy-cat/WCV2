Abstract
Recent person re-identification (ReID) leverages hetero-
geneous sensing with multiple modalities and viewpoints
to improve robustness across diverse conditions. How-
ever, most approaches target predefined scenario pairs (e.g.,
visible-infrared or aerial-ground) and train separate task-
specific models. In contrast, real-world systems often query
against galleries that cover all scenarios, making such de-
signs inefficient and complex to deploy. To bridge this
gap, we introduceAny-Scenario ReID (AS-ReID): given
a query from any (modality, viewpoint) scenario, a sin-
gle model retrieves the same identity from a heterogeneous
gallery spanning all scenarios. Progress toward AS-ReID
is limited by two factors: (i) the lack of a real-world-
aligned benchmark with broad scenario coverage, and (ii)
the challenge of learning representations with strong intra-
identity cohesion and inter-identity discrimination under di-
verse scenarios. To this end, we constructWHU-MARS, a
multispectral aerial-ground benchmark with 2,337 identi-
ties and 434,620 images captured by RGB, near-infrared,
and thermal infrared cameras on both ground and UAV
platforms. WHU-MARS spans day-night, multiple seasons,
and diverse weather, enabling AS-ReID as well as conven-
tional ReID tasks. We further propose theUnified Align-
ment and Discrimination (UAD)framework. Progressive
Center Alignment (ProCA) aggregates multi-view features
into modality centers and then aligns them toward identity
centers to reduce scenario bias. Global Prototype Discrimi-
nation (GPD) contrasts samples against global identity pro-
totypes to enforce large-margin discrimination. Extensive
experiments highlight the challenges of WHU-MARS and
demonstrate the effectiveness of UAD on AS-ReID.

While effective within their own settings, these tasks are
organized around predefined scenario pairs (e.g., visible-
infrared or aerial-ground) with task-specific models and
evaluation protocols. In real-world applications, however,
a query may come from any modality and any viewpoint,
and the gallery typically contains all available scenarios.
Such pairwise designs fragment retrieval across scenarios
and hinder learning or evaluating a single model that works
universally. This raises a fundamental question: I) Can we
move beyond predefined scenario pairs and establish a uni-
fied retrieval paradigm that works across any scenario?
As summarized in Table 1, most existing datasets are
built around predefined scenario pairs. VI-ReID datasets
contain only RGB-NIR or RGB-TIR, while AG-ReID
broadens viewpoints but lacks modality variety. Datasets
that include both cross-modal and cross-view imagery [10,
29] typically provide limited per-identity scenario cover-
age and are evaluated by splitting into scenario-pair subsets,
which hinders learning unified, scenario-invariant represen-
tations. Furthermore, daytime-centric collection undersam-
ples nighttime conditions where infrared sensors are most
valuable, limiting all-day robustness evaluation. Thus, II)
How to build a benchmark that captures diverse scenarios
and real-world complexity to support a unified ReID task?
On the methodology side, many approaches address pre-
defined scenario pairs with multi-branch architectures [41,
57] or pairwise alignment losses [3, 44]. As modalities
and viewpoints grow, such designs rely on scenario-specific
modules or pairwise objectives, inflating parameters and
scaling quadratically with scenarios, which is ill-suited for
any-scenario settings. Moreover, heterogeneous sensing
in AS-ReID introduces large appearance gaps across sce-
narios, while mixing all scenarios in a single gallery dra-
matically increases visually similar but semantically dif-
ferent distractors. As a result, AS-ReID poses two in-
tertwined representation challenges:(a) scenario-invariant
intra-identity cohesion, where features of the same identity
should cluster across scenarios in a unified space; and(b)
large-margin inter-identity discrimination, where features
should maintain clear global margins amid abundant hard
negatives from heterogeneous galleries. Accordingly, III)
How to design a single, scalable framework that learns ef-
fectively from heterogeneous sources while simultaneously
enforcing (a) and (b) for any-scenario ReID?
For problem I), we introduce a new task namedAny-
Scenario Person ReID (AS-ReID). Here, ascenariorefers
to a combination of modality and viewpoint from heteroge-
neous sensors. The task requires that, given a query cap-
tured in anyscenario, a single model retrieves the same
identity from a heterogeneous gallery that spans images
from all availablescenarios. AS-ReID subsumes traditional
single-scenario, cross-modal, and cross-view cases within a
single task, thereby avoiding the closed-world assumption
of training separate models for predefined scenario pairs
and providing a unified, deployment-aligned training and
evaluation paradigm.
For problem II), we contribute a large-scale multispec-
tral aerial-ground benchmarkWHU-MARS. WHU-MARS
comprises 2,337 identities and 434,620 images captured by
RGB-NIR-TIR sensors on both ground and UA V platforms,
with tri-spectral, cross-view coverage for each identity, en-
abling unified training and evaluation for AS-ReID. WHU-
MARS covers day and night, multiple seasons, and diverse
weather, yielding broad variations in illumination, view-
point, scale, and spectral characteristics that reflect real-
world diversity. In addition, we provide a paired-only sub-
set with synchronized tri-spectral samples, enabling consis-
tent evaluation of AG-, VI-, MM-, and AS-ReID settings on
the same identities. To the best of our knowledge, WHU-
MARS is the largest multispectral person ReID dataset to
date and provides a solid foundation for AS-ReID.
For problem III), we propose aUnified Alignment
and Discrimination (UAD)framework to address two core
challenges in AS-ReID:(a) Progressive Center Alignment
(ProCA)consolidates view diversity into modality centers
and aligns them toward identity centers to strengthen sce-
nario consensus.(b) Global Prototype Discrimination (G
PD)complements ProCA by injecting global structure, con-
trasting samples against global prototypes to impose large
margins in the representation space. UAD trains a single,
scenario-invariant model without extra branches or pairwise
objectives and achieves strong performance in AS-ReID.

2. Related Work
2.1. Person Re-Identification Datasets
Across existing ReID tasks, numerous benchmarks exist, as
summarized in Table 1. For VI-ReID, SYSU-MM01 [37]
and RegDB [27] support visible-infrared all-day retrieval,
while LLCM [56] and AT-USTC [22] emphasize chal-
lenging nighttime and long-term settings. To incorporate
aerial viewpoints, PRAI-1581 [53] and UA V-Human [20]
provide UA V-captured imagery, and AG-ReID.v2 [28]
and G2A-VReID [54] extend to aerial-ground matching;
CARGO [52] provides large-scale synthetic aerial-ground
data. MP-ReID [10] and AG-VPReID.VIR [29] broaden
coverage for cross-platform cross-modality evaluation, and
RGBNT201 [59] offers tri-spectral paired samples for MM-
ReID. A complementary line composes or extends existing
datasets to widen scenario coverage: MMReID-Bench [17]
aggregates multiple ReID tasks and datasets, and recent
augmentations add sketches [2, 63] or textual descrip-
tions [5, 13, 14] to classic ReID benchmarks. Collectively,
these datasets provide solid task-specific foundations.
Despite spanning diverse scenarios, most datasets fol-
low predefined scenario pairs and are evaluated via pair-
specific splits, which constrain unified training and any-
to-any evaluation. Cross-source compositions introduce
domain and protocol shifts and rarely provide consistent
multi-scenario observations per identity. They also remain
largely daytime-centric, undersampling nighttime condi-
tions. These factors motivate a benchmark for real-world
applications: consistent identity observations across modal-
ities and viewpoints under day-night, seasonal, and weather
variations, enabling unified any-to-any retrieval and com-
patible with standard VI-, AG-, and MM-ReID protocols.
2.2. Multi-Scenario ReID Methods
Recent efforts aim to model diverse scenarios within a
single network. Prompt-based approaches such as Uni-
Prompt [10], Instruct-ReID [12], and VersReID [60] learn
scenario- or task-specific prompts to guide multi-scenario
retrieval. Unified-representation methods [16, 42, 63] en-
code heterogeneous modalities in a shared space. Task-
focused extensions broaden coverage: Uni-AT [22] tar-
gets anytime retrieval with scenario-specific class tokens;
MixER [1] and MixReID [23] extend VI-ReID to both
cross- and intra-modality retrieval; MCL [45] explores un-
paired USL-VI-ReID; and MDReID [7] and UCPL [62]
support modality mismatch and cross-modality retrieval in
MM-ReID. Together, these lines of work extend multi-
scenario modeling via prompt conditioning, unified repre-
sentation, and task-specific protocol extensions.
Despite this progress, current approaches remain in-
sufficient for AS-ReID. VI-style solutions often adopt
multi-branch architectures or pairwise objectives, result-
ing in complexity that grows quickly as scenarios increase.
Prompt-based systems commonly train separate models for
predefined scenario pairs or on heterogeneous datasets with
disjoint identities, which limits learning identity-consistent
features across scenarios. Unified-representation pipelines
are optimized with RGB-anchored objectives, favoring vis-
ible galleries rather than symmetric any-to-any matching
across modalities and viewpoints. Protocol extensions that
attach separate heads or scenario-specific retrieval rules fur-
ther partition the feature space and complicate scaling to
heterogeneous galleries. These limitations motivate a sim-
ple but effective framework that learns a unified, scenario-
irrelevant representation with a single model for AS-ReID.

3. The WHU-MARS Dataset
3.1. Dataset Overview
We introduce WHU-MARS, a large-scale multispectral
aerial-ground ReID dataset designed for AS-ReID, which
also supports standard AG-, VI-, and MM-ReID protocols.
Data were collected on a university campus using two UA V
platforms and five ground camera nodes. The UA V plat-
forms carried DJI H20T cameras at altitudes between 20-
50m, while the ground nodes employed custom tri-spectral
cameras at about 1.5m. Both platforms synchronously
recorded RGB, NIR, and TIR video streams. The collection
spanned from July to the following January between 09:00
and 21:00 local time, covering both daytime and nighttime
and capturing diverse seasonal and weather conditions. Fig-
ure 2 shows representative samples highlighting the diver-
sity of WHU-MARS. WHU-MARS contains 2,337 identi-
ties and 434,620 images across 13 sessions, totaling over
38 hours of video. Each identity appears in all three modal-
ities and multiple views. As summarized in Table 1, to our
knowledge, WHU-MARS is the largest multispectral per-
son ReID dataset to date. Data collection was authorized by
the institutional office and conducted in publicly accessible
areas. On-site notices were posted during capture, and all
images underwent automatic face mosaicking and contain
no personally identifiable metadata to protect privacy. Addi-
tional device specifications, annotation procedures, and de-
tailed statistics are provided in the supplementary material.
3.2. Dataset Features
Scale and Tri-Spectral Coverage.WHU-MARS com-
prises 2,337 identities and 434,620 images, and every iden-
tity is observed in RGB, NIR, and TIR across views, en-
abling AS-ReID and supporting standard AG-, VI-, and
MM-ReID protocols within a single benchmark.
Day-Night Imaging.The collection spans both day-
time and nighttime. Diurnal variations cause pronounced
changes in illumination and background, increasing re-
trieval difficulty and encouraging models to learn time-
invariant identity information.

ReID) task, then present a unified single-branch baseline
tailored to this setting, and finally introduce the Unified
Alignment and Discrimination (UAD) framework. UAD
targets the two challenges highlighted in AS-ReID: (i)
scenario-irrelevant intra-identity cohesionand (ii)strong
inter-identity discriminationunder large heterogeneous gal-
leries. Figure 3 illustrates the overall architecture.

5. Experiment
5.1. Experimental Settings
Datasets.All experiments are conducted on the WHU-
MARS introduced in Section 3. Unless otherwise specified,
we use the WHU-MARS-1000 as the default and addition-
ally use the WHU-MARS-2337 to assess scalability.
Evaluation Protocols.We report CMC (Rank-k) scores
and mAP for all tasks. Following the standard VI-ReID
protocol, we additionally report mean Inverse Negative Pre-
cision (mINP) [47] for VI-ReID only.
Implementation details.We adopt Vision Transformer [4]
(ViT-Base) backbone pre-trained on ImageNet and train
with Distributed Data Parallel (DDP) on four NVIDIA RTX
3090 GPUs. Images are resized to128×256and augmented
with padding, random resized cropping, horizontal flipping,
and random erasing [61]. We employ aP×M×Ksampler
withP=16identities,M=3modalities, andK=4images,

5.2. Experimental Analysis
In this section, we present a comprehensive evaluation
on WHU-MARS across four settings, including AS-ReID,
AG-ReID, VI-ReID, and MM-ReID, and analyze the impact
of our key design choices on performance.
Any-Scenario ReID.We evaluate a range of recent gen-
eral ReID methods under the AS-ReID protocol on WHU-
MARS. Heterogeneous galleries that mix modalities and
viewpoints make AS-ReID substantially more challenging
than conventional settings. As detailed in Table 3, standard
CNN and Transformer based methods such as BoT [25]
and TransReID [11] achieve Rank-1 below 20% on WHU-
MARS-1000. Human-centric pre-training and stronger
backbones improve robustness: TransReID-SSL [26] and
CLIP-ReID [18] consistently outperform other generic
methods. AG-ReID methods that explicitly learn view-
point invariance can partially transfer their benefits to AS-
ReID and bring further gains, although cross-modality dis-
crepancies still cap performance. Restricting evaluation
to the ground-daytime subset WHU-MARS-1000-GD im-
proves all methods, indicating that excluding nighttime
and aerial viewpoints eases retrieval. However, the task
remains nontrivial because modality gaps remain signifi-
cant. Scaling from WHU-MARS-1000 to the larger and
more diverse WHU-MARS-2337 leads to a consistent drop
across methods, reflecting the increased number of hard
negatives and broader domain shifts with more identities
and scenarios. Across these settings, our UAD achieves
the best overall performance and uses the fewest params
among Transformer-based methods, indicating that learn-
ing a unified representation in a single model is feasible and
competitive for AS-ReID while remaining practical for de-
ployment. We attribute the gains primarily to ProCA and
GPD, which jointly enforce scenario-invariant cohesion and
global discrimination under heterogeneous galleries.

5.3. Ablation Study
We conduct ablations under the AS-ReID protocol to as-
sess the contribution of each component in UAD. As shown
in Table 8, introducing ProCA leads to consistent improve-
ments, indicating that progressive alignment strengthens
scenario-irrelevant learning. Enabling GPD produces larger
gains by injecting the global prototype structure. Combin-
ing ProCA and GPD achieves the best overall performance,
confirming the intended synergy: ProCA progressively reg-
ularizes the batch identity centers and stabilizes the proto-
type memory, while GPD leverages them to sharpen global
discrimination under heterogeneous galleries.
5.4. Visualization
To further examine how UAD shapes the representation
space, we visualize the cosine similarity distributions of
intra-identity and inter-identity pairs in Figure 4. Compared
with the baseline, which exhibits substantially concentrated
and overlapped distributions under scenario heterogeneity,
UAD widens the mean similarity margin between the two
distributions, indicating a more separable and discrimina-
tive representation space for robust retrieval.
6. Conclusion
We introduce Any-Scenario ReID (AS-ReID), a unified and
deployment-aligned paradigm in which a single model re-
trieves identities from a heterogeneous gallery across di-
verse scenarios. To support this task, we contribute WHU-
MARS, a multispectral aerial-ground dataset with tri-
spectral, cross-view coverage for each identity, supporting
AS-ReID as well as conventional AG-, VI-, and MM-ReID
protocols. Methodologically, our Unified Alignment and
Discrimination (UAD) framework strengthens scenario-
invariant intra-identity cohesion through Progressive Cen-
ter Alignment (ProCA) and enforces large inter-identity
margins via Global Prototype Discrimination (GPD). Ex-
tensive experiments under multiple protocols highlight the
challenge of WHU-MARS and validate the effectiveness
of UAD. We believe WHU-MARS provides a solid data
foundation for the ReID community, and that AS-ReID
and UAD represent concrete steps toward more general and
deployment-friendly person ReID systems.
